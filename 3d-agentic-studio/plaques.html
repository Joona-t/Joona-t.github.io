What you are looking at

Construction

A flat-backed plaque with a recessed, two-colour face

Each plaque is a single square slab, 4 mm thick, with a flat back so it can lean on a shelf or sit in the stand. The stencil artwork is cut 1 mm into the front face. At the bottom of every cut a thin floor in the second colour is printed as a separate mesh, so the engraved lines read as colour, not just as shadow.

In the yellow/red files the body is yellow and the floors are red. The two meshes share the same stencil outline: the body is “plaque minus artwork” and the floor is “artwork, 0.24 mm thick”, so they never overlap (the build script asserts an intersection volume below 0.001 mm³).

The stand is a separate prototype

A 140 × 55 × 15 mm block with a 124 mm long, 4.5 mm wide, 10.5 mm deep slot for a 4 mm plaque. It has not been fit-tested on a real print, so the stand preview in the viewer only shows the intended placement.

Technical questions

About these files

Are these files ready to print?

They are prototypes. All 25 yellow/red 3MFs pass a Bambu Studio CLI slice with two PLA Matte profiles and no warnings, and the geometry is watertight. No plaque has been printed yet, so detail merging, adhesion and the stand fit are unverified. Open the file in Bambu Studio, inspect the fine lines in the preview, and treat the first print as a test.

Why 225 mm and not 240?

The Bambu A1 bed is 256 × 256 mm, and a two-colour print needs a prime tower on the same plate. The first 240 mm plaques collided with the tower; 230 and 227 mm size tests failed the CLI slice with “G-code conflicts detected”. At 225 mm, with the tower narrowed to 20 mm and placed at (2, 200), every design slices clean. Fitting by dimensions alone is not enough: the tower and its brim need room too.

What is the prime tower, and why does it matter here?

When a single-nozzle printer switches filament, it purges the old colour into a sacrificial tower before continuing, so the colour change is clean. That tower occupies bed space next to the model. With a plaque almost as wide as the bed, the tower position decides whether the plate is printable at all. The signed 195 mm plaque leaves more room; its current file was sliced clean with a 35 mm tower.

How deep is the engraving, and what is the red floor?

The artwork is cut 1 mm into the 4 mm face. The floor of every cut is a separate 0.24 mm thick mesh in the second colour, sitting from 2.76 to 3.0 mm above the back. Sliced at 0.2 mm, the floor becomes one red layer (layer 15), then the printer switches back to the body colour for the final walls of the recess.

Is the orange colour in the viewer realistic?

No. The viewer draws every colourway as an opaque matte surface. Translucent orange filament transmits and scatters light, so the printed look will differ. The orange/yellow 3MF files also still carry the PLA Matte placeholder profile; select the real spool's profile in Bambu Studio and reslice before printing.

What is different about the signed concept 11?

It is a 195 × 195 × 4 mm version of concept 11 with the artist name Tyrninoksa engraved in katakana, ティルニノクサ, in the bottom-left frame. It was built by its own script from the same stencil, with the signature kept inside the 8 mm frame away from the portrait. The 225 mm original stays available as a separate version.

Are the engraving sources exact copies of the concept art?

No. Every stencil is a reinterpretation drawn so that it prints: thicker lines, simplified shading, no gradients. Use the compare panel in the studio to see where each one departs from its concept.

Which layer height and nozzle were used in the checks?

0.2 mm layers, 0.4 mm nozzle, the “0.20mm Standard @BBL A1” print profile, 15% sparse infill, no supports. The build script compensates line width for a 0.4 mm nozzle; a finer nozzle would allow thinner detail but would need the stencils rebuilt.

Can I sell plaques made from this?

Not from this artwork: it is fan art of characters that belong to their rights holders. The code is MIT, so a version with your own designs is yours to do what you like with.

About this site

A one-off guide to creating things with your own AI agent, with 3D printing as the example. Made by Joona (Tyrninoksa), designing together with AI agents.

Fan art: many pieces here show characters I don't own (Code Geass, Bleach and others). They are made for fun, not official, and not for sale. AI images: concept pictures marked “AI-generated concept” were made with an image generator; prints and photos are real.
